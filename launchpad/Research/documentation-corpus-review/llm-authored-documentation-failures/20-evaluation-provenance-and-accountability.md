## Executive answer

Agent-authored documentation should carry enough evidence to reconstruct **what artifact
was produced, for which task and revision, from which sources, through which relevant
actions, with which unsupported inferences and uncertainties, under whose authority, and
with what validation and human decision**. The record should be machine-readable enough
to query and human-readable enough to review. It need not preserve private chain-of-
thought or every token; it must preserve decision-relevant evidence and known limits.

W3C PROV supplies durable primitives: entities, activities, agents, derivation, revision,
quotation, primary sources, responsibility, and time
([PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/)). SLSA provenance shows a software-supply-chain
pattern for binding an output to build inputs and process metadata
([SLSA provenance v1.2](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.2/provenance)). C2PA shows how content
history and AI actions can be represented and cryptographically bound, while explicitly
warning that provenance may be incomplete and does not determine whether content is true
([C2PA 2.2 explainer](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/explainer/Explainer.html)).
These are useful models, not direct mandates for Markdown documentation.

Provenance must therefore remain separate from assurance. A trace can show that a report
was generated by a named workflow from a signed commit and approved by a reviewer. It
cannot show that sources were sufficient, inferences were valid, the reviewer understood
the system, or omitted cases do not exist. Model-card and datasheet proposals similarly
aim to improve transparency through structured context, intended use, evaluation, and
limitations, but large-scale observation of 32,111 model cards found that limitations and
evaluation were among the least-filled sections
([Mitchell et al., 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03993);
[Pepe et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05160)). A field's existence is not evidence
that it is complete or candid.

Quality should be measured as a **profile**, not one score: claim support and accuracy;
task success; executable correctness; subject/task/risk coverage; temporal freshness;
cross-document consistency; findability and comprehension; security/privacy; provenance
completeness; and review effectiveness. Report denominators, strata, uncertainty, escapes,
and unmeasured risks. NIST's AI RMF calls for context-relevant quantitative, qualitative,
or mixed methods, uncertainty, regular evaluation, documented generalization limits, and
explicit treatment of risks that cannot be measured
([AI RMF Core](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/)).

Accountability requires a named owner and a recorded decision, not merely an “AI-generated”
label or approval badge. The owner must be empowered to narrow, reject, correct, and retire
the document, and the record must distinguish agent contribution, automated validation,
human review scope, and unresolved risk. Aggregate status may support triage, but it must
never erase a failed high-consequence stratum or imply truth beyond the underlying checks.

## Question, scope, and method

### Subquestions

- Which provenance facts are necessary to reproduce or audit a documentation change?
- At what granularity should sources, transformations, claims, inferences, and review be
  connected?
- How should model contribution be disclosed without retaining sensitive or misleading
  implementation detail?
- What does provenance prove, and what does it leave unverified?
- Which quality dimensions and denominators avoid a misleading scalar assurance claim?
- How should responsibility, exceptions, corrections, retention, and privacy be handled?

### Exclusions

- A requirement to expose hidden chain-of-thought, secrets, private source contents,
  personal reviewer data, proprietary prompts, or provider internals.
- A universal claim about legal disclosure duties. Applicable law, contracts, and sector
  regulation require separate jurisdiction- and use-case-specific analysis.
- Blockchain as a necessary provenance mechanism.
- A program-wide synthesis or adoption of a repository schema.

## Failure taxonomy

| Failure class | Description | Evidence and boundary conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Source opacity | The artifact names no decisive sources, revisions, selection process, exclusions, or unavailable evidence. | Prevents audit and freshness checks. Full source capture may be unsafe or impossible; identifiers and access-controlled records can substitute for content. |
| Activity opacity | Tool calls, searches, transformations, skipped inputs, command failures, truncation, and validation scope are absent. | W3C PROV can represent activities and used/generated entities; it does not dictate which events are material for this workflow. |
| Inference laundering | A conclusion derived by the agent appears indistinguishable from a directly observed fact. | A claim ledger can label observation, source statement, calculation, interpretation, and recommendation. Labels do not validate the inference. |
| Uncertainty flattening | Unknown, disputed, version-dependent, inaccessible, or weakly supported claims become uniform declarative prose. | NIST calls for uncertainty and generalization limits in measurement; calibrated claim confidence remains difficult. |
| Model-contribution ambiguity | Readers cannot tell whether the model drafted, transformed, selected evidence, ran tools, or made a substantive recommendation. | A binary AI label is too coarse; exact provider internals may be unavailable and model names may be mutable aliases. |
| Review theatre | “Human reviewed” lacks reviewer role, questions asked, evidence inspected, decision, exceptions, or unresolved risks. | Presence of an approver proves a workflow event, not competence or correctness. |
| Provenance-as-truth | Valid signatures, hashes, commits, or lineage are treated as factual assurance. | C2PA explicitly states that valid provenance does not determine truth and may be incomplete. |
| Trace without integrity | Metadata can be edited independently of the artifact or self-reported by the same process it is meant to audit. | Binding and independent collection improve tamper evidence; compromised producers can still sign false assertions. |
| Overcollection | Traces retain secrets, personal data, private prompts, restricted source content, or unnecessary reviewer surveillance. | Provenance itself creates security/privacy risk; minimization and access control are required. |
| Aggregate masking | One score averages over failure classes, roles, tasks, components, or rare high-consequence cases. | Hidden-stratification research shows strong aggregates can hide important weak subsets in another domain ([Oakden-Rayner et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7665161/)). Transfer supports the metric warning, not a documentation defect rate. |
| Metric gaming | Production optimizes page counts, pass rates, citations, or judge scores while unmeasured quality deteriorates. | Any operational metric can redirect effort; direct agent-documentation gaming evidence is sparse. Use balanced profiles and outcome audits. |
| Accountability diffusion | Model provider, tool builder, agent operator, author, reviewer, and document owner each assume another party is responsible. | W3C PROV can attribute agents and activities; organizational authority and remedy duties must still be assigned. |

## Causes and mechanisms

### Provenance granularity is mismatched to decisions

A commit-level record is cheap but cannot show which source supports a safety warning. A
sentence-level graph can become costly, invasive, and brittle under rewriting. The useful
unit is risk-scaled: artifact-level provenance for all changes; claim-level lineage for
material behavior, policy, security, destructive operations, limits, and quantitative
claims; and step-level traces only where reproduction or incident analysis needs them.

### Self-reporting creates circular assurance

If the same agent writes the document, claims it checked every source, scores itself, and
emits the provenance record, those artifacts share one failure channel. Application-level
capture of tool identity, revision, exit status, and content hashes is stronger than
free-form narration. Independent human or deterministic checks are still needed for
semantic support.

### Metadata fields invite false completeness

Templates improve comparability but can be filled with generic text or omitted. The
model-card corpus study is direct evidence that reporting artifacts in practice often
leave evaluation and limitations sparse. Required fields need validation, while their
substance needs sampling and outcome-based audit. “Not known” is more informative than a
fabricated complete-looking answer.

### Aggregate metrics remove decision context

An average claim-entailment score can hide a false destructive command; a high task-
success rate can hide a failed minority platform; a perfect schema score can coexist with
wrong prose. Oakden-Rayner et al. empirically demonstrate that aggregate model metrics can
mask clinically important weak strata. The general lesson is to preserve task and risk
strata and prevent a good common-case metric from compensating for a failed hard limit.

### Accountability is not identical to attribution

Attribution records participation. Accountability also requires authority, competence,
decision criteria, remedy, and consequences. A reviewer who can only approve, or an owner
who cannot access the sources, cannot discharge meaningful responsibility even if their
identity appears in metadata.

## Detection and validation

### Proposed minimum provenance record

This is a candidate design derived from the evidence, not an established standard.

| Record group | Minimum fields | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Artifact identity | Repository, path, content hash, base and output revision, creation/update time, intended audience and task. | Hashes establish identity/integrity, not truth. |
| Authorized task | Request identifier or bounded task text, governing instruction versions, allowed sources/actions, publication destination. | Avoid retaining sensitive prompt content; record a protected reference or digest where necessary. |
| Source selection | Candidate and selected source identifiers, exact revisions/versions, source class and authority, relevant spans or queries, exclusions, access failures, and selection rationale. | Candidate-set completeness is rarely provable; mark unknown and inaccessible sources. |
| Tool activity | Tool and version, operation, input/output identifiers or digests, environment, status/exit code, truncation, timeout, and retained validation artifact. | Do not store secrets or large raw outputs unnecessarily. |
| Material claims | Claim identifier, text or stable span, claim type, supporting and contrary evidence, inference summary, scope, confidence basis, and unresolved gap. | No private chain-of-thought is required; a concise checkable inference is. |
| Model contribution | Provider/model identifier as observed, agent/workflow version, role in selection/drafting/transformation/review, relevant configuration class, and time. | Model aliases and hosted internals may be mutable or undisclosed; record that limitation. |
| Automated validation | Check/version/configuration, exact scope and environment, pass/fail/skipped/unverified counts, findings, and pass proposition. | A green check proves only its predicate. |
| Human decision | Reviewer identity or accountable pseudonymous role, expertise/authority, review type, evidence sampled, decision, exceptions, unresolved risk, and time. | Approval is not a factual guarantee; minimize personal data. |
| Lifecycle | Owner, next review trigger/date, supersedes/superseded-by links, corrections, incidents, and retirement state. | Calendar freshness alone does not prove continuing validity. |

### Integrity and access

Bind the record to the artifact and immutable source revisions using repository commits,
hashes, signatures, or an attestation system. Preserve append-only corrections where risk
justifies it. Separate public disclosure from protected audit detail: readers may need a
model-contribution statement and evidence links, while raw tool output or restricted
sources remain access-controlled. C2PA is instructive in treating provenance as optional,
privacy-sensitive, tamper-evident history rather than a universal truth label.

### Quality profile

Measure at least these dimensions separately where relevant:

- **Claim support:** sampled material claims entailed by applicable authoritative evidence;
  unsupported, contradicted, and unverifiable rates.
- **Executable validity:** examples/procedures passing specified environments and negative
  tests; skipped and unsafe-to-run counts.
- **Reader task success:** completion, error, time, and recovery for representative roles.
- **Coverage:** declared component, interface, task, lifecycle, decision, and risk strata,
  with partial, private, not-applicable, and unknown states.
- **Temporal coherence:** age since decisive evidence, revision match, change-to-doc
  latency, and stale-claim escapes.
- **Consistency/findability:** authoritative-answer retrieval, contradiction findings,
  terminology and link integrity.
- **Security/privacy:** findings and incident escapes by severity; scanner scope and
  limitations.
- **Review effectiveness:** seeded or adjudicated defect recall, false challenges,
  correction precision, reviewer scope, and unresolved-risk handling.
- **Provenance completeness:** presence and validity of required links and events, reported
  separately from substantive quality.

For every metric, state purpose, unit, denominator, sampling, strata, threshold, revision,
uncertainty, exclusions, and owner. Use hard gates for unacceptable high-consequence
conditions; do not allow averages elsewhere to compensate. Trends and escaped-defect
analysis are more informative than a one-time score, but they still depend on reporting.

## Mitigations

- Capture provenance automatically at the tool/workflow boundary and let authors add
  semantic rationale; do not rely exclusively on retrospective memory or model narration.
- Use interoperable entity/activity/agent and derivation concepts, but adopt only fields
  that support review, reproduction, correction, access control, or accountability.
- Require claim-level evidence for consequential assertions and artifact-level provenance
  for ordinary edits. Record “unknown” and “not checked” as first-class states.
- Bind records to immutable revisions and preserve correction lineage. Re-run validations
  when source, dependency, model, prompt, or environment changes materially.
- Separate model contribution, automated validation, human review, and accountable
  ownership. Never let one badge stand in for the others.
- Publish a multidimensional scorecard with denominators and weak strata visible; prohibit
  a scalar “quality score” from overriding a failed risk gate.
- Audit record substance and downstream outcomes through stratified samples, seeded
  defects, user-task tests, correction latency, and incident review.
- Minimize sensitive trace content, define retention, restrict access, redact safely, and
  log access to protected evidence where warranted.
- Assign an owner with authority and resources to correct or retire the document and an
  escalation path for unresolved evidence or risk.

## Limits and open questions

- W3C PROV, C2PA, and SLSA solve different interoperability and integrity problems. Their
  reuse here is architectural analogy, not proof that one schema fits documentation.
- Rich provenance can impose authoring, storage, privacy, and review cost. Overly detailed
  traces may become unreadable or expose protected inputs.
- Provenance can be honestly incomplete or dishonestly complete. Cryptographic binding
  detects later alteration, not false assertions by an authorized signer.
- Model identifiers, prompts, retrieval services, and hosted tools may not be reproducible.
  Record observed versions and gaps rather than claiming deterministic replay.
- Confidence scores from models are not automatically calibrated probabilities. Prefer
  evidence-based categories and empirical calibration where decisions require numbers.
- Human review metadata may encourage checkbox behavior and cannot prove reviewer
  attention or expertise. Effectiveness must be sampled against defects and outcomes.
- The proposed metric profile still reflects known categories. Unknown harms and reader
  needs require incident learning, qualitative research, and periodic redesign.
- It remains open which provenance granularity gives the best audit value per cost, how to
  represent semantic derivation through large rewrites, and how to compare systems without
  incentives collapsing onto the reported metrics.

## Practical review checks

- Candidate check: confirm the artifact record names repository, path, content hash, base
  and output revisions, task, audience, owner, and lifecycle state.
- Candidate check: for every consequential claim, inspect its authoritative supporting
  source, revision, counterevidence, inference summary, scope, and unresolved gap.
- Candidate check: distinguish sources considered from sources selected and record search,
  access, truncation, timeout, and exclusion limitations.
- Candidate check: capture tool/version, environment, exit status, and validation artifact
  outside the model's free-form narrative where possible.
- Candidate check: state exactly what the model selected, drafted, transformed, or reviewed;
  do not rely on a binary AI label.
- Candidate check: record the human review type, authority, evidence sampled, exceptions,
  and decision; “human reviewed” alone is insufficient.
- Candidate check: verify provenance integrity separately from factual support and show both
  results.
- Candidate check: report quality by task, risk, component, role, platform, and defect class
  with denominators and unknowns; do not publish an unqualified aggregate score.
- Candidate check: prevent a passing average from compensating for a failed security,
  destructive-procedure, policy-authority, or other hard-risk gate.
- Candidate check: test retention and access controls so the audit record does not become a
  new disclosure channel.
