## Executive answer

A citation makes an unsupported claim look supported because **the thing a reader checks
and the thing that would establish the claim are two different objects, and only the
cheap one is ever checked**. A reader — human or machine — checks that a reference is
present, well-formed, and resolvable. Support requires that a real source, at a
determinate version, *says the specific thing asserted*. Each of the four families in the
question breaks a different link of that chain while leaving every checkable surface
intact:

- **Fabricated** citations break referent existence. The reference is well-formed and the
  identifiers are often real — borrowed from other documents — so it survives every check
  short of retrieval.
- **Misapplied** citations break entailment. The source is real, resolvable, and *about
  the subject*; it simply does not say the thing. Relevance is what the checker and the
  skimming reader both mistake for support.
- **Mutable** citations break the binding between claim and version. The reference
  resolved to a supporting source once; nothing re-establishes that it still does, and
  a moving referent produces no error signal when it stops supporting the claim.
- **Circular** citations break independence. Corroboration is counted by *repetition*,
  and repetition is cheap when the repeats descend from one origin — including from the
  citing system's own earlier output.

Three findings qualify that answer materially, and each is stated again in *Limits*:

1. **The citation is not generated by the reasoning that produced the claim.** Mechanistic
   work on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct finds citation emitted by a distributed "attributional
   ensemble" relying on shallow heuristics such as entity co-reference, and reports that
   amplifying those components repairs over 90% of missed citations *without improving
   answer quality* ([van Dort and Heuss 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28358), preprint,
   one open-weights model). If that generalizes, a citation is not weak evidence that the
   model consulted the source; it is close to no evidence at all.
2. **Surface citation quality and actual support are empirically decoupled.** In an audit
   of deep-research agents, link validity exceeded 94% and topical relevance exceeded 80%,
   while factual accuracy against the source content was 39–77%
   ([Onweller et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06635), preprint). The measurable
   surface improves while the property that matters does not.
3. **This is not a purely machine failure, and treating it as one will misdirect the
   remedy.** A 2025 meta-analysis of 46 studies and roughly 32,000 human-written citations
   found 16.9% of quotations inaccurate, about half of them major, with **no significant
   improvement over time**
   ([Research Integrity and Peer Review 2025](https://researchintegrityjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s41073-025-00173-z)).
   Agents raise the volume and change the shape of the failure; they did not introduce it.

The local corpus in this repository already encodes the strongest available structural
answer — a per-claim ledger with `FACT`/`INFERENCE`/`TEAM_KNOWLEDGE` classes, mandatory
SHA pinning, and CI validation — and its own standards state plainly what that machinery
cannot reach. Verified first-hand at the recorded revision: a citation naming a real file
that says nothing on the subject passes validation silently, `Justfile:999999` returns
`ok` against a 1005-line file, and a syntactically pinned GitHub link to a file that never
existed returns `ok` because nothing is fetched. **Structural validation raises the floor
under fabrication and mutability and does nothing at all about misapplication.**

## Question, scope, and method

### Subquestions

- What distinct failure forms do "fabricated", "misapplied", "mutable", and "circular"
  name, and where do they overlap rather than partition?
- By what mechanism does each form survive the checks a reader or a checker actually
  performs — that is, why does it *look* evidence-backed rather than merely being wrong?
- What is specific to LLM agents authoring documentation from repository evidence, and
  what is inherited from long-standing human citation practice?
- Which of these forms can be detected mechanically, which require reading, and what does
  each detection method fail to establish?
- Which mitigations have evidence behind them, and what do they cost or fail to cover?
- What counterevidence bounds the answer — where do these mechanisms not apply?

Triangulation was explicitly tested for circularity, as the contract requires. The
"deep-research agents produce many unsupported statements" finding is carried by at least
three independently constructed audits with different metrics and different system sets
([DeepTRACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04499),
[Onweller et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06635),
[Rao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03173)); the older
"citation ≠ support" finding traces to [Liu et al. 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.467.pdf).
The mechanistic explanation, by contrast, rests on **one preprint and one model**, and is
labelled accordingly throughout rather than being promoted by repetition.

Counterevidence was sought in three directions: the human base rate for the same failure;
evidence that grounding and citation requirements *do* help; and evidence that automated
attribution checking is itself unreliable. All three were found and are reported.

**Stop condition reached.** Searching stopped when new sources produced new *instances*
of the four families but no new mechanism, no new detection class, and no change in
confidence — specifically, when further agent-audit preprints kept re-measuring the same
surface-versus-support gap already established by three independent audits.

## Failure taxonomy

The four families are **not disjoint**. Fabrication and circularity meet at
self-corroboration; mutability decays into misapplication; misapplication is what
fabrication becomes once the referent is real. They are grouped by which link of the
support chain breaks.

| Failure class | Description | Evidence and boundary conditions |
|---|---|---|
| **A1. Wholesale fabrication** | The cited work does not exist. | GPT-3.5 fabricated 55% and GPT-4 18% of 636 citations across 84 generated literature reviews ([Walters and Wilder 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-41032-5), *Scientific Reports*, peer-reviewed). In deep-research agents, 3–13% of citation URLs were hallucinated and 5–18% non-resolving overall, across 10 systems and 221,000+ URLs ([Rao et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03173), preprint). **Boundary:** rates are model- and domain-dependent; the 2023 figures do not describe 2026 systems. |
| **A2. Borrowed identifier** | A fabricated source carries *real* identifiers taken from unrelated real documents, so spot-checks of the identifier succeed. | The fabricated "Varghese" opinion bore docket number 18-13694, which belongs to *George Cornea v. U.S. Attorney General*, and a Federal Reporter citation belonging to *J.D. v. Azar*, 925 F.3d 1291 ([*Mata v. Avianca*, 678 F. Supp. 3d 443 (S.D.N.Y. 2023)](https://www.law.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/archive/2025/12/Mata-v-Avianca-Inc.pdf), ¶28). |
| **A3. Recursive fabrication** | The fabricated source contains its own fabricated citations, so following one hop lands inside the fabrication rather than outside it. | *Mata*, ¶29: the fake decision "includes internal citations and quotes from decisions that are themselves non-existent". |
| **A4. Self-corroboration** | The generator is asked to verify its own output and confirms it; the confirmation is then filed as independent evidence. | *Mata*, ¶45: counsel asked ChatGPT "Is Varghese a real case" and "Are the other cases you provided fake"; it "responded that it had supplied 'real' authorities that could be found through Westlaw, LexisNexis and the Federal Reporter", and the screenshots were annexed as an exhibit. |
| **A5. Fabricated supporting artifact** | Challenged, the system produces the *source document* rather than retracting. | *Mata*, ¶22 and ¶24: excerpts of the "cases" were annexed and had "traits that are superficially consistent with actual judicial decisions", though the reasoning was "gibberish" and quotation marks "often unpaired". |
| **B1. Dead-end citation** | A claim is supported by a source containing no content addressing the claim. | Named and measured in [Greenberg 2009, BMJ](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2714656/): "Support of a claim with citation to papers that do not contain content addressing the claim." |
| **B2. Citation diversion** | Real content is cited but re-described as meaning something else. | Greenberg 2009: "Citing content but claiming it has a different meaning, thereby diverting its implications" — three papers cited data showing minimal β-amyloid as "confirmed" support. |
| **B3. Transmutation** | A hypothesis becomes a fact by being cited as one. | Greenberg 2009: "the conversion of hypothesis into fact through the act of citation alone"; 9 of 37 citations (24%) supporting temporal claims cited papers with no relevant statements. |
| **B4. Relevance mistaken for entailment** | The source concerns the subject without asserting the proposition. | This repository's own standard states the test: "Does the cited source say this, or does it merely concern this? ... It will feel like support, because it is *relevant*; relevance is not entailment" (`launchpad/docs/corpus/standards/evidence.md`). |
| **B5. Decision laundering** | An authorial choice is dressed as a derivation by attaching an on-topic citation that does not compel it. | Same standard: "the choice becomes unattributable. A reader who believes the claim came from the evidence will not go looking for the person who made the call". `ADR-0003` states the same risk for repository attribution: "attributing it to a repository launders it into apparent fact" (read via `git show HEAD:launchpad/decisions/ADR-0003-handbook-page-provenance-contract.md`). |
| **B6. Structural pass mistaken for verification** | A checker confirms a citation *resolves*; a reader reads that as confirmation it *supports*. | Verified first-hand (see *Detection and validation*): `launchpad/docs/corpus/standards/evidence.md` states "a `FACT` citing a real file that says nothing on its subject passes with no notice at all — not an error, not an `UNVERIFIED` line, nothing." |
| **B7. Non-supporting-but-relevant retrieval** | In RAG, the retrieved passage is topically close and cited; the assertion goes beyond it. | Measured as the gap between relevance and factual accuracy: >80% relevance vs 39–77% factual accuracy ([Onweller et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06635), preprint); 51.5% of generated sentences fully supported and 74% of citations supporting their sentence in generative search engines ([Liu et al. 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.467.pdf), peer-reviewed). |
| **C1. Unpinned reference** | The reference names a moving target, so its meaning changes without any edit to the document. | `ADR-0003`: "Pinning a full SHA rather than `blob/main` is what makes staleness detection possible at all — a link to `main` silently changes meaning as `main` moves." Enforced here: `…/blob/main/Justfile` returns `error` (verified). |
| **C2. Pinned but unverified interior** | The reference is pinned and resolvable, but the *position* inside it is never checked. | Verified: `Justfile:999999` returns `ok` against a 1005-line file. The code-references standard states the consequence: "a position that has silently drifted is worse than no position: it looks precise." |
| **C3. Syntactic pin without retrieval** | A correctly-shaped pinned URL is accepted without any fetch, so a pinned link to a file that never existed passes. | Verified: `https://github.com/launchpad-26/buzz/blob/<40 hex>/does-not-exist-anywhere.md` returns `ok`. This is the point where **mutability protection and fabrication protection fail to compose**. |
| **C4. Link rot and content drift** | The external referent disappears or silently changes. | [Zittrain, Bowers and Stanton 2021](https://dash.harvard.edu/handle/1/37367405) examined every hyperlink in *New York Times* articles from 1996 to mid-2019 and found substantial linkrot and content drift, defining drift as "often-unannounced changes such as retractions, additions, and replacements to the content at a particular URL". |
| **C5. Ledger lag** | A whole-document "checked at revision X" marker is advanced while only some claims were re-verified. | `launchpad/docs/corpus/standards/provenance.md` MUST 1–4 exist precisely to forbid this: the recorded-revision entry "has no way to say 'current for these claims, stale for those'." |
| **C6. Environment-dependent verdict** | The same citation verifies or fails depending on the checkout, so a green run is a property of a machine, not of the repository. | **New finding, verified first-hand.** In this sparse worktree, `launchpad/decisions/ADR-0029-corpus-evidence-precedence.md` returns `error` — "does not resolve to a real file in the repository" — while `git cat-file -e HEAD:<same path>` exits 0. See *Detection and validation*. |
| **C7. Movement detected, meaning not** | Staleness tooling reports that cited bytes moved, never that a claim stopped holding — and at this revision most of the corpus has moved. | **Measured first-hand:** `python3 launchpad/project-intelligence/corpus/stale.py` on 2026-09-08 reported `SUMMARY  350 node(s): 304 stale, 18 fresh, 28 unestablished`. `stale.py`'s own docstring: it "detects **movement, not meaning** — a whitespace commit flags a node exactly as a rewrite does", and a `fresh` verdict "does **not** mean any claim in the node still holds". A same-branch commit (`3bbbd8367`) records the converse error: comparing *last-touch commit* rather than *content* flagged three citations stale when "every cited line still pointed exactly where it did". |
| **D1. Citogenesis** | An unsourced claim is repeated by a citable outlet, then cited back to that outlet as its source. | [Wikipedia, *Circular reporting*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circular_reporting) and [Wikipedia:List of citogenesis incidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:List_of_citogenesis_incidents), which sets the inclusion bar at the closed loop, not mere copying. |
| **D2. Amplification** | Reviews and secondary documents carrying no primary data multiply the apparent support. | Greenberg 2009: "expansion of a belief system without data"; one review carried 63% of all 220,553 citation paths, and four papers by one group carried 95%. |
| **D3. Citation bias** | The network omits what refutes the claim, so the surviving citations look unanimous. | Greenberg 2009: supportive primary data received 94% of citations and critical data 6% (P=0.01). |
| **D4. Synthetic-evidence loop** | Retrieval pulls in generated content, which is then cited as external corroboration. | [Yu, Kim and Kim 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16136) (preprint): 67% pool contamination produced over 80% exposure contamination, and — the load-bearing part — "answer accuracy remains stable despite the reliance on synthetic sources", a state the authors call deceptively healthy. |
| **D5. Corpus self-reference** | A generated document cites a sibling generated from the same origin, and the two read as independent. | **Interpretation, not a measured finding.** No study measuring this in a documentation corpus was located. The mechanism is entailed by D1 plus D4 and by this repository's own no-duplication rule ("a copy stays green forever after going stale", `evidence.md` SHOULD 6), but the transfer is inference. Adjacent to topic 15, and left to it. |

## Causes and mechanisms

### Sourced: the citation is not produced by the reasoning that produced the claim

The most consequential mechanistic evidence located is
[van Dort and Heuss 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28358) (preprint; Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct;
PopQA and HotpotQA; activation patching). They locate citation generation in a distributed
"attributional ensemble" of attention heads and MLP layers, find it leaning on shallow
heuristics such as entity co-reference matching, and show that amplifying those components
repairs over 90% of missed citations **without improving answer quality**.

**Interpretation.** If citation and answer are produced by parallel pathways, then the
reader's natural inference — *it cited the source, so it read the source, so the source
says it* — is unfounded by construction rather than by accident. This single result, if it
generalizes, explains why all four families are cheap to produce: a well-formed reference
is a text pattern, and text patterns are what the decoder is for. **Confidence: moderate.**
One preprint, one open-weights model, two short-form QA datasets; long-form documentation
generation from repository evidence is a materially different task and was not tested.

### Sourced: the checkable surface and the property that matters move independently

Three independent audits agree that grounding does not close the gap, using different
metrics and different system sets:

- Generative search engines: 51.5% of sentences fully supported; 74% of citations support
  their sentence ([Liu et al. 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.467.pdf)).
- Deep-research systems: citation accuracy 40–80% across systems, with large fractions of
  statements unsupported by the systems' own listed sources
  ([Venkit et al. 2025, DeepTRACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04499), preprint).
- Deep-research agents: link validity >94%, relevance >80%, factual accuracy 39–77%; and
  factual accuracy fell roughly 42% as tool calls rose from 2 to 150
  ([Onweller et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06635), preprint).

**Interpretation.** The last of these is the sharpest statement of the whole topic: the
metrics a reviewer *can* cheaply compute — does the link work, is it on-topic — are the
metrics that look good. The tool-call finding also suggests that **more agentic effort
makes the surface richer while making support thinner**, which is precisely the condition
under which a document reads as more thoroughly researched than it is. Boundary: one
preprint, and the mechanism behind the tool-call effect is not established.

### Sourced: retrieval does not eliminate the failure, only reduces it

Providers marketed RAG as eliminating hallucinated legal citations. The first
preregistered evaluation found the claims "overstated": Lexis+ AI, Westlaw AI-Assisted
Research, and Ask Practical Law AI hallucinated **17–33%** of the time, reduced relative to
GPT-4 but not eliminated
([Magesh et al., *Journal of Empirical Legal Studies* 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jels.12413);
[preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20362)). This is simultaneously evidence *for*
grounding as a mitigation and *against* grounding as a solution — see *Mitigations*.

### Sourced: structural validation is deliberately, and documented as, form-only

This repository's checker resolves a cited repository path against the filesystem and
never opens it. Its own standard says so in terms this report cannot improve on: the
validator "confirms that a cited repository path resolves to a real file inside the
repository. It never opens that file. Nothing anywhere compares a source against the
`statement` sitting above it" (`launchpad/docs/corpus/standards/evidence.md`).

**Interpretation.** The mechanism by which this *creates* apparent support is a reading
error, not a tooling defect: a green CI run is a fact about form that gets read as a fact
about content. The standard anticipates this too, listing "three things a green run does
not mean". The residual risk is that a reader who never opens the standard sees only the
green check.

### Sourced: repetition is counted instead of independence

Greenberg's network analysis is the cleanest demonstration that authority can be
manufactured by citation structure alone: 242 papers, 675 citations, 220,553 citation
paths, and a claim held to be established with the primary data pointing the other way —
"citation can be used to generate information cascades resulting in unfounded authority of
claims" ([Greenberg 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2714656/)). The
research contract governing this program encodes the same defence in its §4: "repeated
claims derived from one origin are one evidentiary chain, not several independent
confirmations."

**Interpretation.** For agents this is worse than for human authors in one specific way:
a retrieval step returns the *most similar* documents, and near-duplicates descending from
one origin are maximally similar. Retrieval therefore has a structural bias toward
returning one chain and presenting it as several. This is inference from how ranked
retrieval works, not a measured finding, and I found no study measuring it.

### Sourced: the evidence base itself is drifting toward synthetic

[Yu, Kim and Kim 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16136) (preprint) model the ecosystem
limb: contamination amplifies (67% of the pool → >80% of exposure) while answer accuracy
stays stable, so the failure is invisible from the outputs. The training-time analogue is
[Shumailov et al., *Nature* 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07566-y),
which finds that indiscriminate training on model-generated content causes irreversible
defects as distribution tails disappear. **Counterevidence noted:** at least one methodological
critique disputes how far that result extends
([Schaeffer et al. 2024 note](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12954), preprint), and the two
phenomena are distinct — retrieval contamination and training collapse share a shape, not
a mechanism. I treat model collapse as adjacent context, not as support for a claim about
citation practice.

### Interpretation: the economics are asymmetric, and that is the whole problem

Producing a plausible citation costs one short generation. Establishing that it supports
the claim costs a retrieval, a read, and a judgement, per claim, and the cost falls on a
different party at a later time. Every mitigation in the next section is an attempt to
move cost back toward production; none removes the asymmetry. This is the author's
reasoning, offered as reasoning: no source measures the relative cost.

### Interpretation: fluency compounds each family

A well-formatted reference list, consistent citation style, and confident prose are
themselves read as diligence signals. That is topic 13's subject and is not developed
here; it is named because every failure above is *more* effective inside a polished
document, and Greenberg's finding that citation is "a powerful form of social
communication" is the pre-LLM version of the same observation.

### A finding the standards do not record: verdicts are checkout-dependent

In this worktree (`git config core.sparseCheckout` → `true`; `git sparse-checkout list`
returns four paths, not including `launchpad/decisions`), the citation
`launchpad/decisions/ADR-0029-corpus-evidence-precedence.md` returns
`error — does not resolve to a real file in the repository`, while
`git cat-file -e HEAD:launchpad/decisions/ADR-0029-corpus-evidence-precedence.md` exits 0.
Fifty-eight files under that directory are tracked in the index with the skip-worktree flag
(`git ls-files -v` prints `S`).

**Sourced fact:** the same citation to the same committed file yields opposite verdicts in
two checkouts of one revision.

**Interpretation:** a filesystem-resolving citation checker reports a property of a working
tree, not of the repository. The direction observed here is a false *negative* — safe, and
loud. The unsafe direction follows from the same fact and was not observed: any local
artifact present in a working tree but absent from the commit — a generated file, an
untracked scratch file, a file from another branch — would resolve `ok` locally and cite
something no reader can retrieve. `stale.py`'s docstring records a related environment
dependency for CI: "It will report almost every node `unestablished` under CI's depth-1
checkout." **Confidence: high** for the observation, **moderate** for the unsafe-direction
inference, which I did not construct a test for.

### What mechanical staleness detection reported here

Run on 2026-09-08 from the repository root, against `HEAD`
(`4bac39fe512c9b289992951de1222abd61d5790c`), using a throwaway virtualenv supplying
`jsonschema` and `pyyaml`:

```
python3 launchpad/project-intelligence/corpus/stale.py
SUMMARY  350 node(s): 304 stale, 18 fresh, 28 unestablished
```

**Sourced fact:** at this revision, 304 of 350 corpus nodes cite at least one file that has
changed since the node's recorded revision. Individual lines in the captured tail take two
shapes: `STALE  <node>: <path> -- file changed between the recorded revision and head`, and
`UNESTABLISHED  <node>: evidence entry N, citation M -- is a graph-edge or tool-result
citation, which names no openable file` (or "is a commit reference, which names no file
`git diff` can check").

**Interpretation.** This is the mutable-citation family at corpus scale, and it shows the
limit of the method in the same breath. `stale` here means *the bytes moved*, not *the claim
broke*; `fresh` means only that the file-naming citations did not move, which
`provenance.md` MUST 3 route 2 accepts as a substitute for re-reading and SHOULD 1 warns
against preferring. And the `unestablished` lines mark citations that name nothing `git
diff` can check at all — the class for which no mechanical currency signal exists in either
direction. **Confidence: high** that the tool reported this; **no confidence expressed**
about how many of the 304 are substantively wrong, which is exactly the judgement the tool
declines to make. Two other worktrees of this clone were running the same script
concurrently during this session, which does not affect a read-only verdict but is recorded
because the run was not made in isolation.

### Detection methods, and what each cannot establish

| Method | Detects | Cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| Citation-shape validation (`validate.py`) | Malformed, unpinned, free-text, and non-resolving repository references | Whether the source supports the claim; whether a line number is real; whether a pinned URL's target exists |
| URL resolution checking | Fabricated and dead links. `urlhealth` reduced non-resolving URLs by 6–79× to under 1% when models used it for self-correction ([Rao et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03173), preprint) | Whether a *resolving* URL says the thing — the whole misapplied family |
| Content-hash staleness (`stale.py`, and `3bbbd8367`'s content-comparison fix) | That cited bytes moved since the recorded revision | Whether a claim still holds; a `fresh` verdict is explicitly not a correctness verdict |
| Automated attribution scoring (AutoAIS / NLI / LLM-judge) | Gross non-entailment at scale | Reliable per-claim verdicts. [AttributionBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15089) reports even a fine-tuned GPT-3.5 reaching only ~80% macro-F1 on binary attribution, attributing failure to "the model's inability to process nuanced information" and to annotator-model information gaps. AutoAIS is characterized as high-precision, low-recall against human judgement |
| Class-based ledgers (`FACT`/`INFERENCE`/`TEAM_KNOWLEDGE`) | Structurally unsupportable shapes: a `FACT` with no citation, an `INFERENCE` with none, an unattributed `TEAM_KNOWLEDGE` | Whether the class chosen is *honest*. The standard names this the failure "this document is mostly about" and says it is "invisible to every check that exists" |
| Adversarial re-reading by a person | All four families, in principle | Nothing, in principle — but it does not scale, and it is unenforceable. The standard's blunt formulation: "Quoting is the load-bearing part, and it is the part nothing can check" |

**The consolidated negative result.** Every mechanical method above tests a property of the
*reference*. Not one tests the relation between the source and the sentence. That relation
is checkable only by retrieval plus reading — by a person, or by a model whose own accuracy
on the task is around 80% at best and whose errors concentrate exactly on the nuanced cases
that matter.

### Worked failure signature from the primary record

*Mata* supplies the detection signature for deep fabrication, and it is not a citation
check. The fabricated opinions were caught by *reading them*: "Its legal analysis is
gibberish", a plaintiff's name changes mid-document, bankruptcy chapters switch without
explanation, "Quotation marks are often unpaired", and the decision "abruptly ends without
a conclusion" (¶27). **Interpretation:** internal incoherence of the *cited artifact* is
detectable where identifier validity is not — which suggests the checkable signal for
fabricated evidence lies in the source's own consistency, not in its reference format.

## Mitigations

Each mitigation below is stated with what it does not cover, because in this topic the
uncovered remainder is where the failure moves next.

1. **Pin every reference to an immutable identifier.** Enforced here at the shape level:
   `blob/main` is a hard error, and `ADR-0003` gives the reason — "a link to `main`
   silently changes meaning as `main` moves". For code outside a repository one controls,
   [Software Heritage SWHIDs](https://www.swhid.org/specification/v1.1/0.Introduction/) go
   further: the core identifier is an intrinsic cryptographic hash computed from the object
   itself, so it can be recomputed and remains valid even if the origin repository is
   removed or rewritten.
   **Does not cover:** existence. A syntactically perfect pin to a file that never existed
   passes (verified, C3). Pinning defeats mutability and is orthogonal to fabrication.
   **Cost:** pinned links go stale as *links* — the code-references standard's own SHOULD 1
   prefers a bare path to `path:line` precisely because an unverified position "looks
   precise".

2. **Resolve, then read; treat resolution as a floor.** URL-health checking demonstrably
   removes the dead-link layer (6–79× reduction, to under 1%, when the model actually used
   the tool — [Rao et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03173), preprint).
   **Does not cover:** support. A 100% resolution rate is compatible with the 39–77%
   factual-accuracy band measured on agents whose links already validated above 94%.
   **Trade-off worth naming:** improving resolution while leaving entailment unchanged
   *increases* the appearance of rigour without changing the substance — a mitigation that
   makes the failure harder to spot.

3. **Classify claims by how they came to be known, in a machine-readable field.** The
   `FACT`/`INFERENCE`/`TEAM_KNOWLEDGE` ledger forces the author to answer "did I open this,
   reason to it, or get told it", and `ADR-0028` requires the class to be validator-visible
   rather than an adjective in prose.
   **Does not cover:** honesty of the classification, which no check can reach.
   **Trade-off:** the class can itself become laundering — the standard records its own
   ledger failing this test twice before the claim was withdrawn.

4. **Provide a third outcome: withdraw the claim.** The corpus standard's *Three outcomes,
   not two* is the mitigation I judge most underrated: where the sources leave a choice
   open and there is nobody to attribute it to, "**withdraw the claim** rather than
   relabelling it", and record a named gap instead. **Interpretation:** this is the only
   listed mitigation that removes an unsupported claim rather than re-dressing it, and it
   is the one with no mechanical enforcement and a direct cost to the author's word count.

5. **Never let a whole-document freshness marker outrun per-claim verification.**
   `provenance.md`'s MUST 1–4 permit the recorded revision to advance only when *every*
   claim is known to hold there, allow a normalized `git diff` as a substitute route only
   for claims whose citations are all file-naming, and make "do not move it" the safe
   default.
   **Does not cover:** semantic drift under an unchanged file — the diff route "proves
   files did not change; it does not prove nobody discovers, on the *next* read, that one
   of those files always said something slightly different from what the claim quotes"
   (SHOULD 1).

6. **Compare content, not history, when testing staleness.** Commit `3bbbd8367` on this
   branch replaced last-touch-commit comparison with blob-identity comparison, on the
   stated ground that "Staleness is a property of content, not history" — a revert, a
   merge, or a mode change postdates a pin without moving a line.
   **Does not cover:** meaning. `stale.py` reports "movement, never 'the node is wrong'".

7. **Escalate genuine conflicts instead of resolving them.** `ADR-0029` requires an author
   facing two authorities of the same claim type to record the contradiction and leave the
   node `flagged` for a human, and explicitly rejects both a fixed hierarchy and
   latest-timestamp-wins ("recency doesn't establish authority").
   **Trade-off, stated in the ADR itself:** "some nodes stay unestablished/flagged pending a
   human decision, rather than the corpus always producing a confident answer. That is
   accepted as the safer failure mode."

8. **Refuse private evidence as support.** `ADR-0029`'s security clause: where evidence
   cannot be published, "the claim stays unestablished rather than asserted from a source
   that can't be shown." **Interpretation:** unpublishable support is the limiting case of
   an uncheckable citation, and admitting it would make every other control optional.

9. **Break independence assumptions deliberately.** Before counting two sources as
   corroboration, establish that they do not descend from one origin (research contract
   §4). Against synthetic contamination, provenance metadata about *how a source was
   produced* is the only structural defence located, and none of the corpus tooling here
   carries such a field.
   **Evidence gap:** I found no evaluation demonstrating that origin-tracing actually
   reduces circular corroboration in an agent pipeline. This mitigation is reasoned, not
   demonstrated.

10. **Keep human verification where it is decisive, and do not assume it happens.**
    *Mata* found the attorney of record sanctionable for "not reading any case cited in his
    affirmation" (headnote 33), and the human quotation-error meta-analysis shows the
    baseline check rate is poor even among motivated professionals. **Interpretation:**
    "a human reviews it" is a mitigation only if the review is specified as *open the
    source and compare it to the sentence*; unspecified review does not perform that act.

## Limits and open questions

- **Genre transfer is the largest limitation.** Nearly all quantitative evidence here comes
  from scientific literature reviews, general-web QA, and legal research. Documentation
  generated from a repository differs in ways that plausibly cut both ways: the evidence is
  local, retrievable, and machine-checkable (better), and it is also mutable at high
  frequency, position-dependent, and cited by paths that drift silently (worse). **No study
  measuring citation support rates in agent-authored software documentation was located.**
  Every quantitative figure in this report should be read as characterizing a related task,
  not this one.
- **The mechanistic explanation rests on one preprint and one 8B open-weights model.**
  If [van Dort and Heuss](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28358) does not generalize to larger
  or differently-trained models, or to long-form generation, the "citation is a parallel
  heuristic" framing weakens considerably — though the behavioural finding it explains
  (surface/support decoupling) is independently established.
- **A large share of the 2026 agent evidence is unrefereed.** DeepTRACE, Onweller et al.,
  Rao et al., Yu et al. and van Dort and Heuss are preprints. They agree with each other
  and with peer-reviewed 2023–2025 work on direction, which is why they are used; their
  specific numbers should not be treated as settled.
- **The human base rate is genuine counterevidence to a "new failure" framing.** 16.9%
  quotation inaccuracy, roughly half major, with no improvement over time
  ([2025 meta-analysis](https://researchintegrityjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s41073-025-00173-z)),
  and Greenberg's 2009 network showing manufactured authority long before LLMs. The honest
  claim is about **volume, speed, and the fabrication family specifically** — humans
  misapply and circularly cite; they rarely invent the referent.
- **Grounding does work, partially, and that cuts against over-claiming.**
  [Magesh et al. 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jels.12413) found
  RAG tools materially better than GPT-4 while still hallucinating 17–33%. A report that
  treats citation machinery as useless would be as wrong as the vendor claims the paper
  refutes.
- **Unresolved: does more agentic effort make provenance worse?** Onweller et al. report
  factual accuracy falling ~42% as tool calls rose from 2 to 150. If that replicates, it
  inverts the intuition that more research produces better-evidenced documents, and it is
  the single most decision-relevant open question for this program. One preprint, one
  experiment.
- **Unresolved: corpus self-reference (D5) is unmeasured.** I could not find a study of
  generated documents citing sibling generated documents within one corpus. The mechanism
  is reasoned from D1 and D4, and I have deliberately not asserted a rate.
- **Not verified: the AI Hallucination Cases database count.** The site returned HTTP 403
  on 2026-09-08. Secondary reports give figures in the high hundreds to over 1,600 for
  mid-2026; **none is asserted here**, and the *Mata* opinion is used instead because it
  can be read directly.
- **Partly verified: the `stale.py` run.** The summary line was observed (below), but only
  the last 25 lines of per-node output were captured, so the distribution of *causes* behind
  the 304 stale nodes was not examined. Nothing here evaluates whether its verdicts are
  correct — only what it reported.
- **Absence of found evidence is not evidence of absence.** Several boxes above are empty
  because nobody appears to have measured them, not because the failures do not occur.

## Practical review checks

Candidates derived from this research. **Not adopted policy** — they are offered for a
future checklist, and several duplicate rules this repository already enforces.

- For each citation, ask the entailment question, not the relevance question: *does this
  source say the statement, or does it merely concern it?* Read the citation as an
  adversary would.
- Confirm the referent exists by retrieval, not by shape. A pinned URL that has never been
  fetched has been checked by nothing.
- Where a source was reached through a tool, record what was actually opened in the
  statement itself — nothing downstream preserves it.
- Treat any claim resting solely on `unverified` citations as unestablished, whatever its
  class.
- Check for one-hop laziness: if a cited source is itself citing, ask whether the terminal
  claim was ever opened. Fabrication and circularity both hide one hop down.
- Never accept the generating system's own confirmation that its output is real. Re-asking
  the generator is not verification (*Mata* ¶45).
- Before counting two sources as corroboration, establish that they are not one chain.
  Repeated wording across sources is the tell.
- For any external source, ask whether it could plausibly be generated content, and whether
  anything in the pipeline would reveal it if it were.
- Confirm that a whole-document "checked at revision X" marker did not advance past the
  claims actually re-verified in the same edit.
- Prefer a bare path to `path:line` unless the statement also names a symbol a reader can
  compare against; an unverified position looks precise and is not.
- Distinguish a claim that follows from the sources from a choice the author made. If it is
  a choice, attribute it to a nameable person or record — and if there is nobody to name,
  withdraw it and record the gap.
- Re-run any citation check in a clean, full checkout of the committed revision before
  trusting it; a working-tree-resolving verdict describes the machine, not the repository.
- Read a green validation run as "the references are well-formed", never as "the claims are
  supported", and say so wherever the run is reported.
